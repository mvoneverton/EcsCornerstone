How You See Yourself / How Others See You  ·  Alex Rivera
How You See Yourself
Outstanding traits

Your primary self-profile is Catalyst/Vanguard. You are positive — naturally inclined to look at the bright side and find opportunity where others see obstacles. You are persuasive, using your ability to read people and situations to win others over. You are alert, constantly scanning for new developments and possibilities. And you are action-oriented — you do not wait for permission to pursue your ambitions.

You thrive on challenge and variety. You set ambitious goals and pursue them with self-motivation and a willingness to take risks others might avoid. Relationships matter to you deeply, but when the stakes are high you shift into a more focused, directive mode. You are at your best when leading people toward a clear objective, rallying their energy and enthusiasm around a shared goal.

Basic desires and motivation

You are driven by the desire to influence — to have a meaningful impact on the people and situations around you. You want your voice to be heard and your ideas taken seriously. Recognition matters to you not out of vanity, but because it signals your contributions have made a difference.

You prefer action over deliberation, big picture over supporting details, and people over process. You are most energized by creative, fast-moving environments where you can make decisions, take ownership, and see results quickly. Routine, rigid structures, and micromanagement drain you faster than almost anything else.
How Others See You
Outstanding traits

Others experience your pattern as Vanguard/Catalyst. They see someone strong-willed and commanding — a person who enters a room and immediately shapes its direction. They recognize your intelligence and your ability to generate options for any challenge placed in front of you. They have likely been struck by your insistence on quality, and by your skill in organizing diverse thoughts into coherent action.

Your secondary pattern softens your sharper edges. What might otherwise be experienced as demanding is tempered by genuine moments of warmth and encouragement — enough that people tend to experience you as motivating rather than autocratic. They hold themselves to higher standards around you because they know you will.

What others are still learning about you
Your restlessness — the constant search for a new challenge — can read as dissatisfaction or instability to those who don't know you well. Over time, they come to see it for what it is: a relentless appetite for growth. Until they do, they may misread your enthusiasm for new ideas as a lack of follow-through on current ones.

Behavior at Work
Outstanding traits at work

In a work environment, your pattern is Catalyst/Vanguard with a Catalyst score of 790 — the highest-usage range, meaning this pattern is immediately visible to everyone around you. Your natural leadership ability comes to the forefront. You focus well on goals and objectives, always keeping the overall project in mind while persuading and motivating those around you.

Your willingness to take risks serves you especially well at work — it positions you for leadership roles and makes you a credible voice when others are hesitant to act. When the pressure is on, your mind moves fast. Even at your most task-focused, your empathy remains present enough to prevent you from becoming ruthless.

How pressure changes you
Under work pressure you operate in intense bursts — executing at near-frantic pace, then stepping back to reconnect with the people around you. You are a big-picture thinker by nature. When colleagues present long lists of supporting details, you find yourself asking what it all means rather than absorbing each item. This is not a weakness — it is the signature of someone who needs to see the forest first.
Areas for Growth

None of us is without blind spots. The goal of this section is to give you specific, honest information about how your natural patterns may land with others — so you can make informed choices about when and how to adapt.

Independent decision-making
Your independent nature can make it difficult to genuinely absorb the input of others. You may have developed a pattern of deciding on a course of action before seriously weighing perspectives that differ from your own. Under stress, this can tip from confidence into rigidity — and others will notice when it does.
Active listening
Your listening skills, while present, may not keep pace with the speed of your thinking. When someone else is speaking and an idea arrives in your mind, your instinct is to say it — now. This can read as dismissive, even when you are genuinely engaged. The impact is often larger than you intend.
Managing perception
Under stressful conditions, your sharp verbal skills and quick mental pace may produce remarks that land as cutting or dismissive, even when your intent was simply to move things forward. Because you can repair relationships quickly, you may underestimate how much the initial damage lingers for others.
Visible empathy
At your lowest moments, others may experience you as arrogant, impatient, or dismissive of their contributions. The antidote is not to become less confident, but to let your natural empathy be more visible in the moments that count most.

Effective Communication with Others

ECS Cornerstone is built on the understanding that no single communication pattern is better than another — they are simply different. Effective communication means temporarily adjusting your natural Assertiveness and Responsiveness to meet others where they are. As a Catalyst/Vanguard, you are naturally high in both axes. The strategies below help you communicate more effectively when the situation calls for a different approach.

When you need to decrease Assertiveness

  • Ask more, tell less — reframe directives as questions without losing your destination
  • Listen without interrupting — note thoughts mentally and wait for the speaker to finish
  • Allow others to express opinions before you do; even a brief pause signals genuine interest
  • Reduce your pace — physically, verbally, and in decision-making
  • Allow others to assume leadership temporarily; it builds teams that function without you

When you need to decrease Responsiveness

  • Lead with facts — some profiles trust data more than enthusiasm; present numbers before vision
  • Control emotional expression in high-stakes conversations; steadiness signals competence
  • Focus on task completion over relationship maintenance in time-sensitive situations
  • Maintain professional boundaries even with people you genuinely care about
  • Pause before verbalizing — ten seconds of deliberation often produces a more trusted response
Adapting to each Cornerstone profile
When communicating with a Vanguard
Be direct and brief. Lead with the conclusion. Respect their time visibly. Avoid personal tangents. Give them room to make the final call when possible — they need ownership to stay engaged.
When communicating with a Catalyst
Match their energy, but be careful not to let the conversation become all rapport and no resolution. Bring enthusiasm but anchor it in outcomes. Let them talk — and then redirect to the decision.
When communicating with a Cultivator
Slow down. Show genuine interest in them as a person before getting to the task. Avoid ultimatums and pressure for immediate decisions — they need time to process. Avoid raising your voice or expressing frustration visibly.
When communicating with an Architect
Bring data. Be precise. Avoid vague language and big-picture hand-waving — they need specifics to trust a conclusion. Give them time to review before asking for a decision. Acknowledge their thoroughness explicitly.
A final note:   Understanding your ECS Cornerstone profile is the beginning, not the destination. Your natural patterns are genuine strengths. The strategies in this report are tools you pick up when the situation calls for them, then set down again. Use this profile as a starting point for ongoing conversations with your team, your manager, and your coach.
